Why it matters now

Merged portfolios inherit merged data problems

Regal and Rexnord PMC each brought brands with their own part-numbering, attribute conventions and catalog systems. Distributors like Motion, Applied and Kaman list those products side by side and expect one consistent feed. The gap between how the data was built and how the channel consumes it is where listings go wrong.

Distributor syndication

Industrial distributors and buying groups each take a different attribute set. The same gearbox is re-keyed many times.

Brand and segment governance

Dozens of brands across three segments need shared rules with local ownership.

Technical depth

Bearings, gearing and motors carry hundreds of engineered attributes, drawings and certifications that have to travel with the record.

The pattern we see

Where product content usually breaks down

We have not audited Regal Rexnord's systems. What follows is the pattern we see at multi-brand industrial manufacturers after a merger and the questions we would like to check against yours.

Brand-by-brand data

  • Each legacy brand has its own catalog system and attribute conventions
  • Engineering, marketing and the segments each hold a piece of the record
  • A cross-brand question like "which products fit this shaft size" takes a project to answer

Distributor re-keying

  • Every distributor and buying group wants a different attribute set and format
  • Updates reach the ERP first and distributor catalogs months later
  • Errors surface in a distributor's site, where Regal Rexnord cannot see them
